# Afterimage — Marketing & Positioning

A working document of what the app is, who it's for, and how to talk about it.

---

## What Afterimage is

Afterimage is a **disposable camera for groups** — a social photo app where you shoot, wait, and reveal together at sunrise.

You start a **roll** with friends, pick a **film stock** and a **soundtrack**, and shoot blind. No previews. No retakes. No filters. The roll develops in the dark for a set window — one day, two days, five days — and on the next sunrise after the window closes, every shot from every person opens at the same moment for the whole group.

It's the opposite of Instagram. Slow where Instagram is fast. Shared where Instagram is performative. Patient where Instagram is anxious. The point isn't to broadcast — it's to remember together.

---

## What the app actually does

### The core ritual
1. **Create a roll** with friends — name it, pick a window (1/2/5 days), choose a film stock, attach a song
2. **Shoot blind** — through the camera you can see what you're aiming at, but the moment you press the shutter the frame is gone forever (until reveal)
3. **Wait** — the roll develops in the dark. Everyone in the group is shooting, nobody sees anything
4. **Reveal at sunrise** — at the next sunrise after the window closes, the whole roll opens at once for everyone
5. **Talk about it** — comments, reactions, and the memory itself

### Features that make it feel like film, not software
- **Film stocks** with real color grades — Kodak Portra 400, Cinestill 800T, Ilford HP5, Lomo Redscale, Polaroid 600, Fuji Superia
- **Soundtracks** — one song attached to each roll, plays under the reveal cinematic
- **The shutter ritual** — a flash, a blackout, no preview — the frame is gone forever
- **Captions** — the shot's author writes a caption as they shoot; it's printed on the frame at reveal
- **The shoebox** — your personal archive of revealed rolls, presented as an editorial film ledger
- **Maps** — see where every roll was shot

### Features that make it feel like a real social product
- Real-time group chats per roll (comments)
- Friend network with mutual rolls + presence
- Notifications when rolls develop, when you're tagged, when someone joins
- QR + code-based roll invites
- Pull-to-refresh that's a film canister "winding"
- A live activity rail showing what your network is doing

## The brand

### One-line positioning
> *Afterimage is a disposable camera, a shared darkroom, and one ritual every morning.*

### The vibe
**Editorial Luxury.** Warm charcoal, tarnished gold, film red, italic Times New Roman, Bebas Neue display, Menlo edge prints. The app looks like the inside of a darkroom that's been polished by an architecture firm. Every screen breathes — sprockets drift, film grain twinkles, titles flicker like projector lamps.

### Voice
Literary. Anticipatory. Patient. Never SaaS-y. Never "elevate your photos" or "unleash your creativity." The copy treats users like they're old enough to know what film feels like, even if they were born after it died.

**Words we use:** roll, develop, reveal, sunrise, shoot, frame, the darkroom, the shoebox, the reel, sealed, no retakes  
**Words we don't:** content, posts, feed, share, story, engage, community, platform

### The four pillars
1. **Slow** — patience is the medium. The wait is the feature.
2. **Shared** — no solo rolls. The reveal happens to all of you at once.
3. **Cinematic** — every detail is composed. Typography, motion, sound, color.
4. **Honest** — no filters in the moment. No retakes. What was there is what you get.

## Who it's for

### Primary audience
**Gen Z + young millennials, 17–32**, design-conscious, culturally literate, oversaturated by Instagram and exhausted by the algorithm.

They're the kids who buy Polaroid film at $30/pack. They follow Olivia Bee on Tumblr. They have a Lomography camera on their bookshelf. They post once a month and curate hard. They go to concerts and don't film the whole thing.

### Secondary audiences
- **Friend groups looking for a shared ritual** — bachelorette parties, road trips, birthdays, festivals, weddings, reunions
- **Couples** — two-person rolls as a slow-burn intimate diary
- **Bands + creative collectives** — tour rolls, studio rolls
- **College freshmen** — first-week rolls, dorm rolls, that first semester abroad

### Who it's NOT for
- People who want followers
- People who want analytics
- People who edit their photos before posting
- People who film entire concerts
- People who want to be discoverable by strangers

This is a closed-loop product. There's no public feed. There never will be one.

## Positioning vs. competitors

| Competitor | What they do | Where Afterimage wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Curated public feed, infinite filters, algorithm-driven | Afterimage is private, slow, and unfilterable. The opposite premise. |
| **Dispo** | Disposable-camera-style with 24-hour development | Afterimage's window is configurable + always reveals at sunrise, not arbitrary 9am. Stocks, soundtracks, cinematic reveal. |
| **Lapse** | Similar reveal mechanic with daily prompts | Afterimage is group-first, not personal journal. Slower cadence, less gamified, more atmospheric. |
| **BeReal** | One forced photo per day with the algorithm | Afterimage is opt-in shared rolls, not surveillance prompts. Photography, not selfies. |
| **Snapchat** | Ephemeral by default | Afterimage's photos persist forever — they just take longer to appear. Memory, not disappearance. |
| **VSCO / Cinestill app** | Pro film grade tools | Afterimage builds the same grade into the social product itself. The film stock isn't a tool, it's the identity of a memory. |

### The single sentence that beats every competitor

> *Everyone else makes you a content creator. Afterimage makes you a photographer in a group.*

## Launch strategy

### Phase 1 — Closed beta (TestFlight, friends + 50 designers)
**Goal:** Get the core ritual feedback. Find the magic.

- Personal invites only — no public download
- Every tester pairs with one other person and starts a "first roll" together
- Weekly check-in survey: did you forget about it, look forward to it, or check obsessively?
- Make the reveal *the* moment people remember

### Phase 2 — Cultural beta (invite-only, 1000 testers, soft launch)
**Goal:** Build the aesthetic identity in public.

- Each tester gets 3 invite codes — feels like getting into a private club
- TikTok content from real users captures the *reveal moment*
- Partner with 5–10 small accounts (film photographers, indie musicians) for "first roll" content
- The waitlist becomes a thing in itself — design the waitlist page like an album sleeve

### Phase 3 — Public launch
**Goal:** Scale the ritual without losing the soul.

- App Store launch tied to a specific cultural moment (first week of fall, last week before a festival)
- One brand campaign, one tagline, one film
- A "Year in Rolls" recap going public as a feature people want to be in
- Light influencer activations — only with people whose taste matches the app

## Content pillars (for socials, marketing, partnerships)

### 1. The reveal moment
The single most cinematic thing in the app. Capture videos of real users at the sunrise reveal. Slow, atmospheric, music-led.

### 2. Stock + soundtrack pairings
*"This is what Cinestill 800T + Frank Ocean sounds like."* Mood reels that double as product education.

### 3. Group rituals
A wedding roll. A bachelorette roll. A bandmates road-trip roll. Real friend groups, real rolls, real reveal moments.

### 4. Behind the design
Editorial-style breakdowns of the typography, the color palette, the motion language. Built for the design community that will champion it.

### 5. The wait
The hardest sell — make people *want* the waiting period. Content about anticipation. About the photo you took that you forgot you took. About checking the countdown.

### 6. Festival / event partnerships
Roll up to a music festival, partner with the organizers, the festival becomes a roll with a stock + a soundtrack. Reveals at sunrise the day after.

## Key dates & rituals to lean into

The brand is about time, so it should mark time:

- **First sunrise of the year** — global reveal moment, public-facing
- **Summer solstice** — longest roll window of the year
- **Winter solstice** — quietest, most intimate rolls
- **Daylight savings** — surprise reveals when the time shifts
- **Friday nights → Sunday sunrises** — the most natural weekend roll window
- **Festival season** — March-October partnership push
- **Wedding season** — May–October partnership push
- **End of year** — Year in Rolls drops universally on December 31st

## Press & narrative angles

### The big idea
*"What if photography was slow again?"*

### Why now
- Algorithmic fatigue is at an all-time high
- Film camera sales are up YoY for the last 5 years
- Gen Z is rejecting Instagram-as-aesthetic in favor of Tumblr + Substack + Letterboxd
- BeReal proved there's appetite for anti-algorithm photo apps; Afterimage is the slower, more cinematic answer

### Who built it
Solo developer with a design obsession. Built off the back of obsessing over 35mm film, Polaroids, the feeling of seeing a roll come back from the lab a week later, and the frustration of social photo apps that all look the same. The whole app is sample-data on purpose during beta — there's no race to monetize. The ritual comes first.

### Founder narrative beats
- *Wanted to make something my friends would actually use, not "users."*
- *Tired of taking photos at concerts I'd never look at again.*
- *The reveal moment was the whole point — everything else is in service of that.*

## Monetization (long-term, not at launch)

The hard rule: **never sell ads.** Never sell user data. Never algorithmically rank content. The premise breaks if it becomes another attention product.

### Realistic paths
- **Afterimage Pro** — $30/year for: more film stocks, longer windows, exclusive soundtracks, custom film stock creation, higher-resolution exports, physical print integration
- **Official rolls** — branded festivals, weddings, events pay for official rolls
- **Print partnerships** — frame your favorite photo from a developed roll, ships in 5 days
- **Year in Rolls book** — a physical print book of your year, $40, ships in time for the holidays

The conservative monetization is the right one. The premium feel of the product depends on it not being desperate.

## Open questions

Things to figure out before launch:

1. **Discoverability** — how do new users find Afterimage if there's no public feed? (Answer is probably: invite-only forever, like Are.na.)
2. **Friend requests vs. roll invites** — should the social graph and the roll graph be separate? Maybe never have "follow" — only "shared a roll with."
3. **What happens to rolls where one person leaves?** — the wedding-roll-ex-boyfriend problem
4. **Notifications during the wait** — should we ping during the developing window, or stay silent until reveal?
5. **What's the right number of "first roll" partners?** — too many feels desperate, too few feels slow
